What Is a Genus?

A genus is a group of closely related plants that sit inside a family. Members of the same genus usually resemble one another in the shape of their leaves, flowers and fruits, and they often live in similar habitats. The plural of genus is genera. In a scientific name such as Ficus benjamina, the first word is always the genus.

This page introduces the eight genera in our collection. Every family has exactly two genera, and the tree diagrams below show at a glance which genus belongs to which family. The next page then goes down one more level to the individual species.

Genera in Sapindaceae

Nephelium

Family: Sapindaceae

Nephelium is a genus of about 22 to 25 species centred in Peninsular Malaysia, with others reaching Borneo, the Philippines and parts of mainland Asia. The trees are generally fairly small and crooked, and are found as middle-storey trees in lowland evergreen rainforest on hills and ridges.

Family Species

Lepisanthes

Family: Sapindaceae

Lepisanthes is a genus of about 24 to 25 species of trees and shrubs , native to tropical Africa, South and Southeast Asia, Australia and Madagascar. Its flowers are unisexual, and its leaves are pinnately compound and spirally arranged. A few species are used as ornamentals, some have medicinal value, and the fruits of some are eaten.

Genera in Orchidaceae

Paphiopedilum

Family: Orchidaceae

Paphiopedilum, often called the Venus slipper, ranges from India eastward across Southeast Asia to the Philippines. Most species grow in lower montane evergreen or seasonally deciduous forest, often on the shaded forest floor, while a few grow on trees and the rest on the ground or on rocks. Nearly all the Bornean species are naturally rare, and some are threatened with extinction unless their habitats are better protected.

Family Species

Phalaenopsis

Family: Orchidaceae

Phalaenopsis, known as the moth orchids. The plants are monopodial epiphytes, sometimes growing on rocks, with long coarse roots and short leafy stems hidden by overlapping leaf bases.The genus is used extensively in hybridisation and is now one of the most common windowsill plants in the world. Most species are now very rare in the wild because of collecting and habitat destruction.

Genera in Arecaceae

Licuala

Family: Arecaceae

Licuala has about 130 species, with the greatest diversity in Malaysia, Borneo and New Guinea. These are under- to mid-storey fan palms, some with single stems, some with several, and some with no stem at all.The leaves are fan-shaped and pleated with toothed margins, and may be whole or split into wedge-shaped segments; the fruits are small, orange or red, and one-seeded. Most species are sensitive to disturbance, which makes them good indicators of human impact on the forest.

Family Species

Calamus

Family: Arecaceae

Calamus is the largest genus of palms in the world, and it is also the largest of the rattan genera. Most species are climbers with long, slender, flexible stems, though some are erect shrubs or have no visible stem. The word rattan comes from the Malay word rotan, and the cane, which is the stem stripped of its leaf sheaths, is used for everything from bridges and baskets to fish traps and furniture. Most of the best commercial rattans belong to this genus. Heavy trade has led to substantial over-exploitation of wild rattan.

Genera in Moraceae

Artocarpus

Family: Moraceae

Artocarpus is native to South and Southeast Asia, New Guinea and the southern Pacific and comprises about 50 species. They are small to large monoecious trees, and all parts contain white latex. The fruit is an aggregate fruit formed by the enlargement of the entire female flower head.Most species yield fairly good timber, and about a dozen are used in traditional medicine in Southeast Asia; the latex is mixed with wax for batik work.

Family Species

Ficus

Family: Moraceae

They grow throughout the tropical and subtropical regions as shrubs, lianas or trees. Figs are characterised by a unique inflorescence called a syconium, an urn-shaped structure that encloses numerous tiny flowers. Figs are keystone species in many tropical forest ecosystems, and over 1,200 vertebrate species are reported to feed on them.

Key Terms

Genus
A group of closely related species within one family.
Understorey
The shaded layer of plants growing beneath the tall forest canopy.
Latex
A milky sap produced by many plants, including those in Moraceae.
Lip (labellum)
The specialised petal of an orchid flower, shaped like a pouch in slipper orchids.

Did You Know?

Genus names are written in italics and always begin with a capital letter. Because scientists share these names worldwide, Calamus means the same rattan group whether you are in Kuching or in a laboratory abroad.
