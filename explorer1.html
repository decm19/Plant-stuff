What Is a Family?

A family is a large group of related plants that share a common set of characteristics, such as the structure of their flowers or the way their fruits develop. Botanists use families to organise the huge variety of plant life into manageable groups. On this page you will meet four families that are well represented in the forests of Sarawak: Sapindaceae, Orchidaceae, Arecaceae and Moraceae.

Each family below includes a short description, a photograph and a few key facts. Later pages look at the genera and species within each family.

The Four Families

1. Sapindaceae (Soapberry Family)

The Sapindaceae occur in temperate to tropical regions, many in laurel forest habitat, throughout the world. Plants of this family have habits ranging from trees to herbaceous plants to lianas, and usually with compound leaves and small flowers. Many of its members produce juicy, edible fruits. Sarawak's forests contain wild relatives of familiar fruit crops, which makes them valuable for future breeding.

2. Orchidaceae (Orchid Family)

Orchidaceae is one of the largest flowering plant families in the world. Orchids may grow on tree branches or on the ground, and their flowers have a specially shaped lip. Their seeds are tiny and need help from fungi to germinate. Borneo is a hotspot for orchids, and many Sarawak species are protected by law.

3. Arecaceae (Palm Family)

Palms usually have a single unbranched stem topped with a crown of large fan-shaped or feather-shaped leaves. Sarawak has upright palms, swamp palms and climbing rattans. Rattan canes have long been used by local communities for weaving baskets, mats and furniture.

4. Moraceae (Mulberry and Fig Family)

Moraceae is mostly made up of tropical trees whose tissues contain a milky sap. Its best-known genera are Artocarpus and Ficus. Figs are a key food source for birds, monkeys and bats, so they support many other animals in the rainforest.

Key Terms

Epiphyte
A plant that grows on another plant for support without harming it, as many orchids do.
Aril
A fleshy covering around a seed, found in many Sapindaceae fruits.
Rattan
A climbing palm with long, flexible stems used for weaving and furniture.
Keystone species
A species that many other organisms depend on, such as forest figs.

Fun Fact

Sarawak's state flower, the Normah orchid, belongs to the Orchidaceae family. It is a good example of how a single plant can become a symbol of a whole region.
