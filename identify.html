Nephelium lappaceum
Common Name:
Rambutan

About This Plant

Rambutan (Nephelium lappaceum) is a tropical fruit tree native to Malaysia. Its name comes from the Malay word rambut, meaning "hair", after the soft spines that cover the fruit. It grows best in warm, humid lowlands with plenty of rain and well-drained, slightly acidic soil.

Care Tips for This Plant

  1. Water during dry spells

    • Rambutan needs a lot of rain
    • Only water it if there has been no rain for a long time.
    • Make sure the soil drains well so the roots do not sit in water.

  2. Fertilise twice a year

    • Feed the tree after harvest and again near the end of the rainy season.
    • Spread the fertiliser around the base of the tree, but keep it away from the trunk.
    • Give young trees less than older ones.

  3. Prune lightly

    • Only cut back branches that are dead or damaged.
    • Plant the tree somewhere sheltered because strong wind can knock off flowers and fruit.
    • Check the trunk now and then for signs of disease.

Right environment for this plant

  1. Warm and humid

    • Rambutan grows best in the hot, damp lowlands of the tropics.
    • It needs warm weather all year round and does not survive frost.
    • A short dry season is fine, but it should not last long.

  2. Plenty of rain

    • The tree needs heavy rain spread evenly through the year.
    • A long dry spell will reduce flowering and fruit.
    • If the dry season drags on, water the tree to help it keep its fruit.

  3. Rich, well-drained soil

    • Deep, fertile soil with plenty of organic matter suits it best.
    • The ground must drain freely, because the roots do not like standing water.
    • Slightly acidic soil is ideal; very alkaline soil makes the leaves turn yellow.
